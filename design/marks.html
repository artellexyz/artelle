Six directions for the Artelle mark — ink on paper, nothing else. One gets cut.

Plate IRecommended

Horizon

A dot at rest on a line — sun on a horizon, a vessel on a shelf, a mark on paper. The smallest complete composition there is, it reads at any size and belongs to no trend. It also scales into a system: each collection can move its dot along the line.

Apex

An A with its crossbar traded for a point of view — the studio roof over the work. Reads as Artelle instantly; slightly more gestural than Horizon, and the dot gives it a pupil.

Plate III

Triptych

Three panels, the centre one standing taller — an altarpiece reduced to three strokes, and an abstract A besides. Painterly rather than pictorial; at 16px it reads as three brushmarks.

Plate IV

Frame

A work hanging on a wall — the square says gallery, the dot says something is in it. The most literal of the six, which is its strength with print buyers and its ceiling everywhere else.

Plate V

Ell

One open gesture — a drawn ellipse that never closes, the ell in artelle, half an enso. The most hand-made of the six; costs some crispness at favicon size.

Plate VI

Point

A dot held in focus — plate mark, lens, full stop. The purest option after Horizon itself; the ring keeps it from being just a dot, but adds the most machinery of the six.
